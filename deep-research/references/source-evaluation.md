# Source evaluation checklist

Use this when a claim matters and you are unsure how much to trust a page.

## Quick credibility check (answer each in a few words)

| Question | Good sign | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Who published it? | Named org or author with a track record | No author, no "about" page |
| How do they know? | Original data, direct access, primary document | "Reports say", no citation |
| When? | Date visible and recent enough for the topic | No date, or "updated" date with old content |
| Why was it written? | To inform, document or report | To sell, rank in search, or recruit affiliates |
| Can it be checked? | Links to data, methodology, originals | Round numbers with no method |
| Does it agree with others? | Independent sources converge | It is the only place saying this |

## Source types, strongest to weakest (for factual claims)

1. Primary documents: laws, filings, standards, specs, official docs, changelogs,
   datasets, peer-reviewed papers, the original announcement.
2. Official statements from the entity itself (strong on what they did or offer, weak
   on how good it is).
3. Established news outlets and trade press that cite primaries.
4. Recognized experts writing under their name.
5. Community sources (forums, Q&A, issue trackers): good for real-world problems and
   workarounds, weak for general claims.
6. Aggregators, listicles, "top 10" pages, AI-generated content farms: leads only.

## Traps

- **Circular sourcing**: five articles, one origin. Trace to the first.
- **Stale evergreen pages**: "2026 guide" headings on content written years earlier.
  Look for concrete dates inside the text.
- **Vendor benchmarks**: real numbers, chosen conditions. Note who ran them.
- **Survivorship in reviews**: forum threads over-represent problems; marketing pages
  over-represent successes.
- **Unit and scope drift**: monthly vs annual, global vs one country, users vs accounts,
  revenue vs bookings.
- **Machine-translated or regional pages**: confirm the key figure in the original
  language page when possible.
- **Search snippets**: can be outdated cached text or cut mid-sentence. Open the page.

## Recency guide

| Topic | Treat as stale after |
|---|---|
| Prices, plans, quotas, rankings, app store charts | weeks |
| Software versions, APIs, platform rules | 1-3 months |
| Market sizes, statistics, surveys | 1 year (and check the data year, not the article year) |
| Laws, standards | until superseded; check for amendments |
| History, science fundamentals | rarely, but check for retractions |

## Handling disagreement

1. Line up the claims side by side with date, source and definition.
2. Look for the reason: different dates, definitions, samples, regions or incentives.
3. If one side is a primary source and current, prefer it and say why.
4. If you cannot resolve it, report the range and the reason; do not average.
